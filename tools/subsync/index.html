How it works

Three ways to get subtitles

Listen & find hears the film through the microphone for about 15 seconds, works out which film it is, and asks you to confirm before downloading. Search by name lets you pick the release that matches your copy. Or load a .srt file, pasted text or a link yourself, with no account.

Syncing

When the captions open, tap anywhere the moment you hear the line shown and they lock on. Nudge by ±0.1s or ±1s (or the ← → keys). If the film slowly drifts, use Fix drift: tap A on an early line and B on a late one. Alignment is remembered per file.

Translation

Optional, to English or Sinhala. Groq AI (sign in) translates about 40 lines at a time, so a film takes a couple of minutes. MyMemory needs no account but has a small daily quota. Every translated line is saved on this device for offline use. Machine translation is approximate, Sinhala especially.

On an iPad or phone

Open this page in Safari, then Share → Add to Home Screen to launch it full-screen. The caption screen is pure black, so an OLED screen barely lights up. A browser can't hear Netflix or other apps directly, so Tap to sync is the dependable way to line up.

Fix drift (two-point)

Tap A on an early line, B on a late line, then apply — fixes gradual desync.

Language

Machine translation is approximate — Sinhala especially, since it has far less training data than English. Expect to reread the odd line.

Display

The stage is pure black so an OLED screen barely draws power. Turn brightness down in a dark room — it dims the captions without touching the system slider.

Auto-sync

Listens to the movie through the mic and guesses the offset. Often fails on iPad — Tap to sync is the reliable one.
